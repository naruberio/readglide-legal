Scene 3 of 3 · Questions and answers

Practice interview answers out loud, with your notes in front of you

Write the questions you expect as Q: lines and your answers as A: lines. Readglide reads each question aloud in the device's voice, waits until it has finished, and scrolls your answer for you to say. Afterwards it shows how much of your prepared answer you actually said, which lines you skipped, how fast you spoke and where you paused.

The scene

A quiet room · two evenings before a video interview · iPhone at eye level

Picture someone applying for a customer-support job. The answers are written: a short introduction, why this role, a problem they once solved. Knowing them on paper is one thing; saying them out loud, in order, without wandering off or dropping the point that matters, is another. There is nobody free to play the interviewer tonight.

How the two evenings go

  1. First evening · a feel for it

    Before writing anything, a quick try with a ready-made interview.

    In Readglide

    On the script list, below Timer & Bell, tap What are you practicing?, then Job interview. A Sample interview opens: six questions and answers in the app's language, from "Please tell me about yourself" to "Do you have any questions for us?", with your role already set to A.

  2. First evening · their own answers

    Then the real thing: the questions this employer is likely to ask, and the answers as they want to say them.

    In Readglide

    Tap the pencil icon (New Script) and write one line per question and per answer, in the same Q: and A: format:

    Q: Why are you interested in this position? A: I want to use what I have learned from customers to help improve your products. (mention the survey)

    Names before the colon are written in capitals. Anything in brackets is a note to yourself: it is neither read aloud nor scored. Tap Save, then Read Now.

  3. First evening · choosing a side

    The phone takes the interviewer's part; the answers are theirs.

    In Readglide

    At the bottom of the prompter, open the menu with the theater-masks icon (My role) and choose A. The Q lines dim, and Read other lines aloud, in the same menu, is on.

  4. Second evening · the run-through

    "Why are you interested in this position?" The question is spoken, the scroll waits, and the answer comes up to be said.

    In Readglide

    Start recording first: the waveform icon at the top (Practice with audio only) for voice alone, or the video camera icon to see yourself the way the interviewer will. Then tap Play. Each time a Q line reaches the reading position, the scroll stops, the device's voice reads the question, and the scroll carries on with your A lines.

  5. Second evening · looking back

    The answer about the problem they solved felt fine. Did it include the part about the warehouse team?

    In Readglide

    Stop the recording and tap Review take. With A chosen, Take Review notes Scored only A's lines, so only your answers count. Read as written shows how closely you kept to your words and how many lines you covered, Skipped lines lists what was dropped, and Speaking rate and Long pauses show the pace. The time spent listening to a question is not counted as a pause. Fillers and off-script words counts the fillers it heard, such as um and uh, and the words that were not in the script.

  6. Second evening · once more

    The skipped line goes back in, and the next run is compared with this one.

    In Readglide

    Practice history keeps the numbers for each saved script: a chart of recent takes, the change since the last one and the best so far, with New personal best when a take beats it. Only the numbers are kept, on the device.

What Readglide takes care of in this scene

  • Each Q: question read aloud in the device's voice, with the scroll waiting until it has finished.
  • Your own interview, written as Q: and A: lines, with private notes in brackets.
  • A sample interview to try first, in the app's language.
  • Only your answers scored: how closely you kept to them, which lines you skipped, your speaking rate and your long pauses.
  • Fillers such as um and uh, and words that were not in the script, counted for each take.
  • A practice history for every saved script, so each run can be compared with the last.
  • Practice with audio only, or on camera.
