Rehearse a presentation so it fits its time limit

Give your script a time budget in Readglide. As it scrolls, a small display shows green, yellow or red, the time left, and whether you are ahead of or behind plan; one tap on Fit to time sets the speed so the rest finishes on time. After the run, each section's planned and actual time sit side by side. For the full dress rehearsal, Timer & Bell keeps time without a script and rings warning and closing bells.

The scene

A lab desk · the week before a conference · iPhone propped against a laptop

Picture a graduate student with a ten-minute talk at a conference, and a session chair who signals two minutes before the end. The first read-through of the draft ran long. Somewhere among the background, the method and the results there is a minute too many, and it is hard to tell where just by feel.

How the week goes

  1. Monday evening · the script

    The talk is already written in four parts. The script gets one heading line for each.

    In Readglide

    On the script list, tap the pencil icon (New Script), give it a Title and put the text under Script, or bring it in from a text, RTF or PDF file with Import. Start each part with a line that begins with #:

    # Background # Method # Results # Summary

    Tap Save, then Read Now.

  2. Tuesday · setting the clock

    Ten minutes, the same as on the day. No camera needed yet, only the voice.

    In Readglide

    At the bottom of the prompter, open the timer menu (Time budget) and choose 10:00. The budget is saved with the script and shared out across its # sections by length.

    Tap the waveform icon at the top (Practice with audio only), then the microphone button (Start audio recording). The bar notes Audio only · not saved to Photos.

  3. Tuesday · the run

    Halfway through the method, the dot turns from green to yellow, and the display says the talk is behind.

    In Readglide

    Tap Play. The clock starts as the script starts to scroll, after the countdown. While the controls are hidden, a small display shows a colored dot, the time left (… left), and On track, … ahead or … behind. A thin line in the script marks where the scroll should be by now, in the same color.

  4. Tuesday · catching up

    Rather than stop, the run carries on at a pace that lands on ten minutes.

    In Readglide

    Tap the screen to bring up the controls and tap Fit to time. Readglide sets the scroll speed so the rest of the script finishes close to the time left, and keeps that speed until you change it. Tap Play to carry on.

  5. Tuesday · after the run

    Now the question from Monday has an answer: which part is too long?

    In Readglide

    Stop the recording (Stop audio recording) and tap Review take. In Take Review, the Time budget section starts with Whole script and then lists each # section as Planned … · Actual …, with how far ahead or behind the talk was by the end of it. Speaking rate and Long pauses show where the time went.

    Here, the method section is the one that runs over. Two sentences go, and the next run comes in on time.

  6. The night before · dress rehearsal

    One last run standing up, slides on the laptop, no script on screen, timed like the real session.

    In Readglide

    On the script list, open Timer & Bell. In Timer Settings, set Time budget to 10 minutes and Warning Bell to two minutes before the end; set the Third Bell to the length of the questions, five minutes after the end. Test the Bell lets you hear it first.

    Tap Start. Green, yellow and red lights and a large countdown fill the screen, and the stage changes from Within Time to Warning Bell to Time’s Up. The bell rings once for the warning, twice when time is up and three times for the third bell.

What Readglide takes care of in this scene

  • A time budget for each script, from the prompter's timer menu or, in 30-second steps, from Take Review.
  • While you speak: a green, yellow or red signal, the time left, and whether the scroll is ahead of or behind plan, with a marker for where you should be by now.
  • Fit to time: one tap sets the scroll speed so the rest of the script finishes close to your time.
  • After the run: planned and actual time for the whole script and for every # section.
  • Timer & Bell for talks with no script on screen: lights, a large countdown, and one, two or three bells.
  • Rehearsals with audio only stay out of Photos.
