Scene 1 of 3 · On camera

Read a script on camera while you record yourself on iPhone

Open your script in Readglide and turn on the camera: the words scroll over the live preview, and you can set them at the top of the screen, right under the front camera. The script never appears in the video. When you stop, Readglide checks what you said against the script on the iPhone itself, and can save a second copy with your false starts cut out.

The scene

A kitchen table · after closing time · iPhone on a small stand, front camera

Picture someone who makes and sells ceramics and wants a 90-second video introducing a new mug for the shop's social media. There is nobody to hold cue cards, and a sheet of paper on the table means glancing down every few seconds. The Camera app records, but it has nowhere to put the words. The script is already written, in a note on the same iPhone.

How the take goes

  1. Ten minutes before

    The script is copied from the note, all of it, in one go.

    In Readglide

    On the script list, tap Paste and record. The prompter opens with the camera already on; as the line under the button says, the script stays on screen and never appears in the video.

    To keep the script for another day, tap the pencil icon (New Script) instead, paste it under Script or bring in a text, RTF or PDF file with Import, tap Save and then Read Now. In the prompter, the video camera icon at the top turns the camera on.

  2. Setting up the shot

    The words should sit just under the lens, so the eyes stay up while reading.

    In Readglide

    Tap the text-size icon at the top (Display settings) and, under Text window, set Height to Half or One third, then Position to Top — near the camera. The camera preview fills the rest of the screen.

    In the same menu, Video Quality sets the resolution and frame rate. The icons next to the camera switch between the front and back cameras and turn sound on or off.

  3. Take one

    The mug is on the table, the lamp is on. Record, breathe, and the first line comes up right under the lens.

    In Readglide

    Tap the record button (Start recording); the elapsed time shows in red. Then tap Play, or anywhere on the text: after a short countdown the script scrolls at a steady pace. Pinch to make it faster or slower while it runs.

    A Bluetooth remote or foot switch can start and pause the scroll and change its speed, so both hands stay free for the mug.

  4. Cut

    The sentence about the glaze came out tangled, and they said it again before carrying on.

    In Readglide

    Tap the stop button (Stop recording). The video goes straight to Photos. Then tap Review take to open Take Review.

  5. Checking the take

    Was everything said? The price, the dishwasher line, the closing thank-you?

    In Readglide

    Readglide turns the voice into text on the iPhone itself and lines it up with the script. Read as written shows how closely the part you read matched the script, word for word, and how many of its lines you covered. Skipped lines lists anything left out; Speaking rate and Long pauses follow. Practice history keeps the numbers for the next take.

  6. Before posting

    The feed plays videos on mute, and the tangled sentence has to go.

    In Readglide

    Under Retakes, Readglide lists the places where part of the script was read again. Leave on the ones to cut and tap Save video without retakes: a new video goes to Photos, and the original stays as it was.

    Under Script captions, Save 9:16 video with captions makes a vertical copy whose captions are the script's own words, timed to the voice, so every name is spelled the way it was written. Share subtitles (.srt) hands over a caption file instead.

What Readglide takes care of in this scene

  • The script floats over the live camera preview and stays out of the recording.
  • The text window can sit at the top of the screen, close to the front camera, at half or a third of the screen's height.
  • Front or back camera, with or without sound, at the video quality you choose.
  • Every take is checked against the script on the device: how much was read as written, which lines were skipped, the speaking rate and the long pauses. The audio is not uploaded.
  • A copy without the reread parts, a captioned copy in the original shape or 9:16, or an .srt file, all from the same take.
